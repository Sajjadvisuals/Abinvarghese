KFC — Independent Spec Campaign

Every Meal Teaches · Take One First. Come Back Later.

Workflows and Experiments Behind This Short Film

Creative Strategy

Identifying the food-waste problem and discovering the human insight behind it: children learn the value of food through the everyday behaviour of their parents.

Brand Strategy

Selecting KFC and defending its relevance by turning the iconic shared bucket into the campaign’s central creative device.

Concept Development

Developing the creative platform, “Every Meal Teaches,” and the behavioural thought, “Take One First. Come Back Later.”

Creative Direction

Designing a two-visit narrative in which the child’s changed behaviour during the second visit reveals what he learned during the first.

Film Direction

Locking the shot division, performances, screen direction, edit points and camera movements before beginning AI generation.

Image Prompt Engineering

Creating precise Nano Banana instructions for characters, environments, expressions, props, composition and visual continuity.

Video Prompt Engineering

Writing shot-specific Seedance instructions covering performance, movement, timing, camera language and natural background activity.

AI Continuity Direction

Maintaining consistency across faces, costumes, props, food quantities, table geography, screen direction and — most importantly — the continuity of every character’s actions.

Iterative Prompt Engineering

Diagnosing unsuccessful generations, identifying exactly what went wrong and rewriting prompts with more accurate performance, continuity and camera instructions.

AI-Assisted Creative Collaboration

Before generating anything, I explained the complete story, concept and strategic intention to Claude and ChatGPT. Throughout the project, I used them as AI associate directors — not to make the creative decisions for me, but to question, refine and strengthen my decisions.

AI-First Creative Workflow

Combining human strategy and filmmaking experience with Claude, ChatGPT, Nano Banana, Seedance and Adobe software to create one connected end-to-end production workflow.

From the Director’s Chair

I began my career working as an assistant director on commercial film sets. That experience became one of the most valuable parts of this AI filmmaking process.

I generated the video shots in approximately six hours because I knew exactly what each shot required: the continuity conditions, character actions, camera movement and precise cue points needed for the final edit.
